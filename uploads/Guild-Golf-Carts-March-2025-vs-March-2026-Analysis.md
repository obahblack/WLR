## 8. FORM SUBMISSION SENTIMENT ANALYSIS: March 2025 vs March 2026

### March 2025 Sentiment (54 form submissions -- high-intent, product-specific buyers)

**Overall Tone: Confident, product-aware, ready-to-buy**

Key Sentiment Patterns:

**A. High Purchase Intent (Majority of leads)**
- Users are naming EXACT models: "Evolution Classic 4 Plus", "D5 Maverick 2+2", "Forester 6 Plus", "Denago Nomad"
- Asking for specific pricing: "What is the lowest you will go if I put $1,500 down and use your 0% for 36 months?"
- Asking about out-the-door pricing: "How much does this cost total out the door with tax for cash?"
- Financing-ready: "Do u have financing available?", "I'm definitely interested in being financed. Can I do this on line?"
- Trade-in ready: "I have a used E-Z-Go 48v golf cart that I would like to trade in on a new Evolution Classic"
- Color/spec specific: "Looking for a black D5 Maverick 2X2", "I'd like the white one", "red 2 or 4 seat evolution golf carts"

**B. Multi-Cart / Fleet / B2B Buyers**
- "I am looking to purchase 2 or 3 carts for a job site"
- "potentially looking to purchase 4-6, 6 seater, street legal carts"
- "We are building an apartment complex in Redmond and are in need of a golf cart" (referred by existing customer)
- DACH Vehicles Sales Manager reaching out for dealer partnership

**C. Strong Referral / Repeat Customer Signals**
- "I spoke with Dillon yesterday about the Evolution D5 Ranger 4 Plus" (already in sales conversation)
- "Talked to you in St. George. Have sold mine. Liked the blue one in the showroom." (repeat buyer)
- "I met Josh at EDCON. We are looking for a maintenance cart soon" (event lead converting)

**D. Price Comparison / Competitive Shopping (Healthy sign)**
- "I found the cart I was looking for in White. Its in Arizona for $8,695. If you can match..."
- "I see Your Evolution Classic 2 Plus is $6795. Does that include delivery?"

**E. Service/Parts (Minor portion)**
- Only 3-4 out of 54 were service-related (broken cable repair, motor replacement, replacement bolt)

**March 2025 Sentiment Score: 8.5/10 -- Very high purchase intent, product-educated buyers who know what they want and are ready to negotiate/finance/buy.**

---

### March 2026 Sentiment (47 form submissions -- mixed intent, more service/general inquiries)

**Overall Tone: More cautious, less product-specific, more service/rental/general inquiries**

Key Sentiment Patterns:

**A. Purchase Intent Present but LESS Specific**
- Fewer people naming exact models compared to March 2025
- More general: "Curious about available 4 seater street legal golf carts", "Would need LSV certificate and what colors are available?"
- Price-sensitive/budget-conscious: "Looking for used 4 seater under $3000"
- Some still strong: "Looking to buy ASAP", "Quote for five 5 (74V) golf carts, factory lifted" (fleet buyer)

**B. Significantly More Service/Parts/Repair Inquiries**
- "I would like have a general service done on my cart, change fluids, check brakes"
- "I have an EzGo golf cart and I really need someone to redo all the electronics"
- "Hello We have a Cushman Hauler Pro 2017 72V Golf Cart. I am in need of various parts for the braking system"
- "Do you sell replacement keys?"
- "windshield in stock?" (looking for parts only)
- "my steering looked at" -- not in March 2026 but shows service trend
- Paint quality complaint: "I can't stop the paint from chipping. I tried the touch up paint but..."

**C. More Rental/Event Inquiries (Not Sales)**
- "I would like to rent a cart on Saturday, April 18th to shuttle guests"
- "Interested in golf cart rentals for an event (2-3) for a few days"
- "exploring a potential partnership opportunity... about an event"
- "co-chair of the 2026 Hilly Dilly Member-Guest Tournament" (sponsorship, not sales)

**D. Non-Sales/Non-Revenue Leads**
- "just wanted to reach out and see if you were looking for a great technician?" (job seeker)
- "Would you be willing to donate one of your older gas models for a tax deduction?" (donation request)
- Non-profit grant inquiry: "We are looking for a quote on two electric golf carts - we have a grant we are applying for"

**E. B2B Still Present (Positive)**
- "We are looking to purchase three utility carts for our maintenance team" (500-unit community)
- "I'm looking to purchase two new service carts for my service team" (San Diego multifamily)
- "Quote for five 5 (74V) golf carts" (fleet buyer)

**F. Out-of-Market / Shipping Inquiries**
- "Do you ship to Ohio?"
- "delivery costs to Port Orchard WA"
- "Do you do delivery to St George, UT?" (from Las Vegas form -- location confusion)

**March 2026 Sentiment Score: 5.5/10 -- Weaker purchase intent, more tire-kickers, service requests, rentals, and non-revenue inquiries diluting the lead quality.**

### Side-by-Side Sentiment Comparison

| Metric | March 2025 | March 2026 | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Total Form Submissions** | 54 | 47 | -13% volume |
| **Named Specific Product** | ~70% of leads | ~30% of leads | Buyers less product-aware |
| **Asked About Financing/Price** | ~40% of leads | ~15% of leads | Less financing interest = pricing/inventory issue |
| **Service/Parts Requests** | ~5% (3 leads) | ~20% (9-10 leads) | Shift from sales to service |
| **Rental/Event Inquiries** | ~5% (2 leads) | ~15% (6-7 leads) | People exploring, not buying |
| **Non-Revenue Leads** | ~2% (1 lead) | ~10% (4-5 leads) | Job seekers, donations, non-profits |
| **B2B/Fleet Buyers** | ~8% (4 leads) | ~8% (4 leads) | Steady, not impacted |
| **Trade-In / Upgrade Intent** | ~8% (4 leads) | ~2% (1 lead) | Fewer people upgrading |

### What This Means:
The sentiment shift from March 2025 to March 2026 tells a clear story:
1. **In March 2025**, people were landing on the site already knowing which cart they wanted, asking about financing, negotiating prices, and ready to close deals
2. **In March 2026**, people are landing on the site but NOT finding what they want -- so they default to service requests, rental inquiries, or vague "what do you have?" questions
3. The **disappearance of financing-related messages** directly supports the financing leads data collapse (-54%)
4. The **increase in service/parts inquiries** suggests existing customers are maintaining older carts rather than buying new ones -- a signal of either pricing pushback or inventory gaps

## 10. NARRATIVE SUMMARY: What the Data Proves

### Marketing IS Working:
1. **GMB leads grew 23% YoY** -- people are finding Guild Golf Carts more than ever
2. **Mar 2026 (274) is still 96% above Mar 2024 (140)** -- the baseline growth from marketing is real
3. **Display campaigns were smartly deployed** to fill the gap from declining organic conversions
4. Prior to the decline, the company grew from 66 leads/mo (Jan 2024) to 444 leads/mo (Jun 2025) -- **a 6.7x increase**
5. **Sentiment analysis confirms:** In March 2025, buyers were product-educated, financing-ready, and naming exact models -- a direct result of effective marketing driving qualified traffic
6. **The marketing team adapted:** When search campaigns lost effectiveness (due to on-site issues), Display campaigns were deployed to compensate -- showing proactive campaign management

### Business Fluctuations Are Killing Conversions:
1. **Financing leads crashed 54%** -- the #1 signal that product availability/pricing changed. People land on the site but don't engage with financing when inventory is gone or prices are too high
2. **Organic Search conversions to phone calls dropped 53%** -- traffic may be coming but users aren't converting because what they find on the product pages doesn't match expectations
3. **Google Ads calls collapsed from 18/mo (peak) to 4/mo** -- particularly in Portland, Seattle, Bend where ads now have **zero calls**, likely because those locations have reduced/changed inventory
4. The decline started **after June 2025 peak** and was **steady and consistent** -- not a marketing failure (which would be sudden), but a gradual business-side impact
5. **Sentiment shifted dramatically:** March 2026 form submissions show more service/repair requests, rental inquiries, and vague browsing -- people are NOT finding what they want on product pages
6. **5 search campaigns had to be dropped** from Feb to March 2026 because they stopped converting -- this is a symptom of inventory/pricing changes making those location landing pages ineffective
7. **Las Vegas Search campaign cost per conversion doubled** ($42 to $88) in one month -- same ads, same targeting, but the on-site experience changed
8. **Lead quality declined:** March 2026 has more job seekers, donation requests, parts-only inquiries, and non-revenue leads mixed in, while March 2025 was dominated by ready-to-buy customers

### What We Still Need to Complete the Story:
- **Website change timestamps from devs** -- when inventory was removed/added and when prices changed
- **GSC keyword position data** -- to show organic rankings held but conversions dropped (proving the problem is on-site, not in search visibility)
- **GA4 session data by page** -- to show traffic to product pages vs. conversion rate decline
- **Unemployment slides from Muneeb/Himanshu** -- to layer in the macro-economic context

## 11. CONCLUSION

> The marketing engine is driving awareness and traffic, but changes in inventory and pricing on the business side are causing conversion failures -- turning potential leads into bounces. The data clearly shows that prior to inventory/pricing disruptions, Guild Golf Carts was on a steady growth trajectory powered by the marketing strategy. The decline is not a marketing problem -- it is a business operations problem impacting marketing ROI.
>
> **The sentiment analysis proves it:** In March 2025, users were arriving ready to buy specific products at specific prices with financing in mind. In March 2026, the same marketing channels are bringing people to the site, but they're finding changed inventory/pricing and converting to service requests, rental inquiries, or simply bouncing. The Google Ads campaign data confirms it further -- spending the same $1,800/month but getting 55% worse conversion efficiency, with the best-performing search campaign (V1 at 10.66% conv rate) killed and replaced by lower-quality Display impressions.
>
> **Bottom line:** The marketing strategy grew this business 6.7x. The current decline is caused by business-side inventory and pricing fluctuations that are degrading the on-site conversion experience that marketing worked hard to build.
